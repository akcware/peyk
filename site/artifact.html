Your data, your call.

It asks before it remembers

When you connect a service it looks at the basics first: who writes to you, what your days look like. Then it tells you what it noticed and asks whether to keep it. Nothing about you is stored without a yes.

Nothing leaves without approval

Every outgoing message and every document it writes waits for your Send. An old button can't send a draft you changed since.

Leaving takes one sentence

Say "delete my account". It asks twice to be sure, then removes everything: your messages, your memory, and the connections to your services.

Set up in one conversation.

  1. 1

    Say hi

    Message the bot. It introduces itself and asks who you are.

    hi
    Hi, I'm Peyk. I watch your mail and calendar and only interrupt when it matters. Who am I talking to?
  2. 2

    Answer one question

    Your name and where you are. That sets your timezone, your budget and your own memory.

    Alex, in Berlin
    Nice to meet you, Alex. Berlin time it is. Want me to connect your Gmail and Google Calendar?
  3. 3

    Tap the link

    It sends a Google sign-in link and waits until the account is connected. Nothing is typed into the chat.

    yes
    Here's your Gmail login: Sign in with Google I'll tell you when it's linked.
    Gmail connected. I'm watching now.

No commands. No passwords in chat. Anyone can message the bot; each person gets their own budget, memory and timezone.
